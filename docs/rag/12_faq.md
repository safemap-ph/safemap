# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is my report really anonymous?

Yes. Reports are anonymous by default. Even if you provide your name or contact information, admin officers are required to remove all personal details before the report is made public on the map.

## How do I track my report?

Go to "Track Report" in the app menu and enter the reference code you received after submitting your report. The reference code is in the format SMPH-XXXXXX (for example, SMPH-A1B2C3).

## What does "Pending Review" mean?

Your report has been received and is waiting for an admin officer to review it. Pending reports are not visible on the public map.

## Why can I not see my report on the map?

Only reports that have been approved or verified by an admin officer appear on the public map. Reports with "Pending Review" or "Dismissed" status are not shown to the public.

## What is the difference between Approved and Verified?

- Approved (for Awareness): The report appears as part of the heatmap for general awareness. It has not been officially confirmed by PNP.
- Verified (PNP Confirmed): The report has been officially confirmed by the Philippine National Police. It appears as an individual marker pin on the map and is included in official statistics.

## What should I do in an emergency?

Call 911 immediately. Do not use the SafeMap app for emergencies that need immediate response. You can also call PNP at 117 or text 0917-847-5757.

## How do I find the nearest police station or hospital?

Open the "Get Help Now" section from the bottom navigation bar. You can tap "Find Nearest WCPD Desk" for the closest station. You can also check the map for nearby hospital and police markers.

## Can I submit a report for someone else?

Yes, you can submit a report on behalf of someone else. Make sure to include accurate details about the incident, including the location and what happened.

## What types of incidents can I report?

You can report: Theft/Robbery, Assault, Fraud/Scam, Harassment, Vandalism, Accidents, Fire, Flooding, Suspicious Activity, Violence, and Other incidents.

## What does the heatmap show?

The heatmap shows areas where approved (but not yet PNP-verified) incidents have been reported. Darker or redder areas indicate higher concentrations of incidents. Blue areas indicate lower concentrations.

## How are the hotspot colors determined?

Colors are based on incident density and severity. Red means critical/high danger areas, orange means assault/violent areas, yellow means medium risk, blue means low risk, and purple means harassment. The heatmap gradient goes from blue (low density) to amber (medium) to red (high density).

## Who reviews my report?

Authorized admin officers (DWSD staff) review all submitted reports. They check if the report is reasonable, remove personal details, check for duplicates or spam, and decide whether to approve, verify, or dismiss it.
